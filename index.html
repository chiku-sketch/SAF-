Core Vision & Objectives

S.A.F. (Solar Air Flying Car) is an advanced electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) vehicle designed under the ArionZ Advanced Mobility initiative. It bridges the gap between personal automotive luxury and high-performance aerospace engineering.

Key Pillars

  • Solar-assisted energy integration (Obsidian-X).
  • Battery-electric VTOL propulsion architecture.
  • Autonomous AI flight assistance & safety monitoring.
  • Lightweight carbon-fibre & titanium composites.

Long-Term Mission

To pioneer a sustainable, safe, and intelligent personal air mobility ecosystem that operates without traditional airport runways.

Vehicle Design & Passenger Cabin

The vehicle features a low-profile (~1.2m height), wide aerodynamic body built to eliminate ground friction while optimizing airflow.

Passenger Capacity (4-Seater)

  • 1 Pilot / Driver seat with smart digital dashboard.
  • 3 Passenger seats in an enclosed, climate-controlled luxury cabin.

Glass Canopy & Landing Skids

  • Full Glass Split Canopy: Center split-line roof mechanism allowing doors to slide seamlessly into the body frame.
  • Landing Skids: Thick (5-10cm) helicopter-style landing skids ensuring stable ground contact without wheels.

Propulsion & Obsidian-X Solar System

Advanced power distribution designed for smooth vertical lift, hovering, and horizontal cruise flight.

Lift & Thrust Architecture

  • 4 Underbody Ducted Fans: Enclosed vertical lift units for stable hovering and VTOL transitions.
  • 2 Rear Thrust Ducts: High-efficiency horizontal boosters for high-speed forward flight.

Obsidian-X Solar Roof

  • Integrated lightweight solar surface targeting 35% efficiency.
  • Supplements primary battery systems and powers auxiliary electronics.

Development Roadmap (6-Phase Plan)

Phase 1: Concept & CAD

Exterior styling, procedural 3D model generation (STL/DXF), component layout, and SolveSpace integration.

Phase 2: Digital Engineering

CFD aerodynamics analysis, structural simulation, energy modeling, and flight-control testing.

Phase 3: Physical Mockups

Scale exterior models, cabin ergonomics testing, propulsion mockups, and solar panel placement.

Phase 4 to 6: Flight & Commercial

Aerospace engineering review, certified subsystem testing, experimental aircraft trials, and commercial scaling.
